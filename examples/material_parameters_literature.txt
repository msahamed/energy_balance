================================================================================
RHEOLOGICAL PARAMETERS - OLIVINE FLOW LAWS
================================================================================

Reference: Hirth, G., & Kohlstedt, D. (2003). Rheology of the upper mantle
and the mantle wedge: A view from the experimentalists.
In Inside the Subduction Factory (pp. 83-105).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DISLOCATION CREEP (Power-law, dominant in mantle)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

General Form: ε̇ = A * σⁿ * exp(-(E + PV)/RT)

Where:
  ε̇ = strain rate
  σ = differential stress
  n = stress exponent
  E = activation energy
  P = pressure
  V = activation volume
  R = gas constant (8.314 J/mol/K)
  T = temperature (K)

DRY OLIVINE (Oceanic Lithosphere/Slab):
  Stress exponent (n):         3.5 ± 0.5
  Activation energy (E):       530 kJ/mol = 5.3 × 10⁵ J/mol
  Activation volume (V):       14-18 cm³/mol (often use 0 for simplicity)
  Pre-exponential factor (A):  1.1 × 10⁵ MPa⁻ⁿ s⁻¹

  Viscosity at 100 km depth:   ~10²¹ Pa·s (cold slab)

WET OLIVINE (Mantle Wedge):
  Stress exponent (n):         3.5 ± 0.5
  Activation energy (E):       480 kJ/mol = 4.8 × 10⁵ J/mol
  Activation volume (V):       11 cm³/mol (often use 0)
  Pre-exponential factor (A):  1.6 × 10³ MPa⁻ⁿ s⁻¹

  Viscosity at 100 km depth:   ~10¹⁹ Pa·s (10× lower than dry)
  Wedge can be 10-100× lower:  ~10¹⁸-10¹⁹ Pa·s

STANDARD VALUES FOR MODELING (Hirth & Kohlstedt 2003):
  visc_exponent:               3.5
  visc_activation_energy:      5.0 × 10⁵ J/mol (500 kJ/mol)
  visc_activation_volume:      0.0 (or 1.0-1.5 × 10⁻⁵ m³/mol if included)
  min_viscosity:               1 × 10¹⁹ Pa·s (wet) or 1 × 10²⁰ Pa·s (dry)
  max_viscosity:               1 × 10²⁴ Pa·s

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DIFFUSION CREEP (Linear, dominant at small grain sizes)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DRY OLIVINE:
  Stress exponent (n):         1.0
  Activation energy (E):       375 kJ/mol = 3.75 × 10⁵ J/mol
  Grain-size exponent (p):     3

WET OLIVINE:
  Stress exponent (n):         1.0
  Activation energy (E):       335 kJ/mol = 3.35 × 10⁵ J/mol
  Grain-size exponent (p):     3

Note: Diffusion creep typically less important in subduction zones
      where stresses are high (dislocation creep dominates)

================================================================================
ELASTIC PARAMETERS
================================================================================

Reference: Turcotte & Schubert (2014), Geodynamics

MANTLE (Olivine-dominated):
  Bulk modulus (K):            50-70 GPa = 5.0-7.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa
  Shear modulus (G):           30-80 GPa = 3.0-8.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa
  Young's modulus (E):         ~160 GPa
  Poisson's ratio (ν):         0.25-0.30

OCEANIC CRUST (Basalt/Gabbro):
  Bulk modulus (K):            50-60 GPa = 5.0-6.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa
  Shear modulus (G):           30-40 GPa = 3.0-4.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa

STANDARD VALUES FOR MODELING:
  bulk_modulus:                5.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa (50 GPa)
  shear_modulus:               3.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa (30 GPa)

================================================================================
THERMAL PARAMETERS
================================================================================

Reference: Turcotte & Schubert (2014), Clauser & Huenges (1995)

MANTLE (Peridotite):
  Density (ρ₀):                3300 kg/m³ (upper mantle)
                               3400 kg/m³ (lower mantle, 410+ km)
  Thermal expansion (α):       3.0 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹
  Heat capacity (Cp):          1000-1200 J/kg/K
  Thermal conductivity (k):    3.0-4.0 W/m/K
                               Temperature dependent: k = k₀ + A/T
                               k₀ = 0.73 W/m/K, A = 1293 W/m

OCEANIC CRUST (Basalt/Gabbro):
  Density (ρ₀):                2900-3000 kg/m³
  Thermal expansion (α):       2.8-3.0 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹
  Heat capacity (Cp):          1000 J/kg/K
  Thermal conductivity (k):    2.0-2.5 W/m/K

SEDIMENTS (if included):
  Density (ρ₀):                2500-2700 kg/m³
  Thermal conductivity (k):    1.5-2.5 W/m/K
  Heat capacity (Cp):          1000 J/kg/K

STANDARD VALUES FOR MODELING:
  rho0:                        3300 kg/m³ (mantle), 3000 kg/m³ (crust)
  alpha:                       3.0 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹
  heat_capacity:               1000 J/kg/K
  therm_cond:                  3.3 W/m/K (mantle), 2.5 W/m/K (crust)

================================================================================
PLASTIC PARAMETERS (Mohr-Coulomb)
================================================================================

Reference: Ranalli (1995), Byerlee (1978)

GENERAL CRUST:
  Cohesion (initial):          10-40 MPa = 1.0-4.0 × 10⁷ Pa
  Cohesion (final):            1-4 MPa = 1.0-4.0 × 10⁶ Pa
  Friction angle:              30° (Byerlee's law)
  Internal friction coef:      μ = tan(30°) ≈ 0.58

MANTLE:
  Cohesion (initial):          40-100 MPa = 4.0-10.0 × 10⁷ Pa
  Cohesion (final):            4-10 MPa = 4.0-10.0 × 10⁶ Pa
  Friction angle:              30° (can be 15-35°)

WEAK ZONES (faults, shear zones):
  Cohesion:                    0.1-1 MPa = 1.0-10.0 × 10⁵ Pa
  Friction angle:              5-15° (very weak)

STRAIN WEAKENING:
  Plastic strain range:        0.0 to 0.1-0.5

DILATION:
  Dilation angle:              0° (volume conserving, typical for deep processes)

STANDARD VALUES FOR MODELING:
  cohesion0:                   4.0 × 10⁷ Pa (40 MPa) - initial
  cohesion1:                   4.0 × 10⁶ Pa (4 MPa) - final
  friction_angle0:             30° (initial)
  friction_angle1:             30° (final, or 15° for weakening)
  dilation_angle0:             0° (no dilation)
  dilation_angle1:             0°
  pls0:                        0.0 (start of weakening)
  pls1:                        0.1 (end of weakening)

TONGA SUBDUCTION ZONE SPECIFIC VALUES
================================================================================

Reference: Peng et al. (2024), JGR Solid Earth; Tonga tomography studies

SLAB CHARACTERISTICS:
  Age:                         120-140 Ma (old, cold)
  Convergence rate:            15-23 cm/yr (fastest worldwide)
  Dip angle:                   45-60° (steep)
  Temperature at 100 km:       ~700-900 K (very cold)

RECOMMENDED FOR TONGA MODEL:
  # Use standard Hirth & Kohlstedt values
  visc_exponent:               3.5 (nonlinear rheology essential)
  visc_activation_energy:      5.0e5 J/mol (500 kJ/mol)

  # Strong viscosity contrast
  Slab min_viscosity:          1e20-1e21 Pa·s (cold)
  Wedge min_viscosity:         1e18-1e19 Pa·s (wet, 100× weaker)

  # Thermal
  Initial slab temp:           Cold (120 Ma plate age)
  Surface temperature:         273 K (0°C)
  Mantle temperature:          1573 K (1300°C)

CONVERSION FACTORS
================================================================================

Energy:
  1 kJ/mol = 1000 J/mol = 1.0 × 10³ J/mol

Pressure:
  1 MPa = 10⁶ Pa = 1.0 × 10⁶ Pa
  1 GPa = 10⁹ Pa = 1.0 × 10⁹ Pa

Volume:
  1 cm³/mol = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ m³/mol

================================================================================
NOTES
================================================================================

1. The visc_coefficient parameter in DynEarthSol is code-specific and needs
   to be calibrated to match the desired reference viscosity.

2. For thermal runaway studies, use:
   - Realistic activation energies (5.0 × 10⁵ J/mol)
   - Strong temperature dependence
   - Cold slab (120 Ma age)
   - Fast convergence (8-23 cm/yr)

3. Nonlinear rheology (n = 3.5) is ESSENTIAL for realistic subduction:
   - Allows extreme viscosity variations (10¹⁸ to 10²⁴ Pa·s)
   - Enables slab bending and corner flow
   - Produces realistic stress distribution

4. Strain weakening is important for:
   - Localizing deformation at plate interfaces
   - Forming realistic shear zones
   - Allowing plate decoupling

5. When in doubt, use Hirth & Kohlstedt (2003) parameters - they are the
   standard in the geodynamics community.
